I. Introduction

Accurate trajectory prediction is a key technique in autonomous vehicle control systems. In a standard pipeline, models are given sensor data of the environment, identify obstacles, and forecast their future positions or velocities. Furthermore, downstream tasks and hardware availability impose hard constraints on the performance and size of the model. This is a complex problem that has received significant attention, as effective obstacle avoidance is the difference between a safe and unsafe autonomous navigation system.

We introduce a model that enables accurate trajectory prediction for lightweight machinery using a single RGB sensor. Our work consists of the following key features:

  • Anytime querying: We introduce an anytime query method that allows our model to meet downstream-defined latency requirements.
  • Probabilistic trajectory modeling: We model future trajectories as a mixture of Gaussians, enabling an explicit and structured representation of multimodal uncertainty.
  • Ability to reason about interactions between agents: We design the model infrastructure to allow the model to consider pairwise interactions between agents.

Although early stoppage, probabilistic trajectories, and agent-interaction modeling have each been studied individually, prior work has not jointly combined all of these components into a single pipeline.

II. Related Works

Classical approaches to trajectory prediction often rely on state estimation techniques such as Kalman filters, which assume linear dynamics and Gaussian noise, but struggle with complex, multimodal behavior. Consequently, neural networks have become widely used in the field of trajectory planning.

Additionally, with the rise of autonomous driving, a multitude of contests and datasets for object prediction have been created, including the Waymo Open Motion Dataset and related benchmarks.

This has led to many new state-of-the-art algorithms that address the challenge by outputting multimodal trajectory predictions. Specifically, our model is inspired by the following two areas of work:

Mixture Density Networks: Mixture Density Networks (MDNs) are models that output mixtures of Gaussians. These approaches have been applied to trajectory prediction and related sequence modeling tasks. However, training them has been shown to be difficult in practice due to their tendency to collapse into a single mode. As a result, several alternative training methods have been proposed, including a “best-of-N” loss function, where only the best prediction is considered. This encourages the model to predict a diverse range of trajectories, an approach that we adapt in our work.

Diffusion Models: Diffusion-based trajectory prediction methods model future trajectories through iterative denoising processes and have recently demonstrated strong performance on multimodal motion forecasting tasks. These methods provide flexible generative modeling capabilities for complex trajectory distributions.

III. Methodology

Our Anytime Trajectory Model pipeline is displayed above. For each frame fed into the model, the algorithm computes a hidden state, which is then reused when the next frame arrives.

Anytime Trajectory Model

Uniquely, we achieve anytime prediction through iterative refinement steps. At every frame, the model continuously refines its output until a trajectory is requested. At that point, the hidden state is passed through the trajectory predictor to generate an estimate.

If a new frame arrives before querying, the process is interrupted and the hidden state is reused together with the new frame. This enables a tradeoff between latency and accuracy: earlier queries produce faster but less accurate predictions, while later queries produce more accurate but higher-latency outputs.
